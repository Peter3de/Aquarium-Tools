How to use these tools

Five tools that work together. Start with the water: the Reef Water Tracker checks your test results and Reef Water Chemistry explains how calcium, magnesium and alkalinity work together and works out doses. Then plan what lives in it: the Reef Coral Guide, Reef Fish Guide and Reef Invert Guide each cover common species and let you keep photos of your own.

Quick start

  1. Open the Reef Water Tracker and choose the coral group that matches your tank.
  2. Check your tank size and type in your latest test results.
  3. Follow the numbered list under What to do, starting at number 1.
  4. Select Save to log so the tracker can learn how fast your tank uses calcium, alkalinity and magnesium.
  5. Open Reef Water Chemistry to see how calcium, magnesium and alkalinity interact, and to work out doses for the products you use.
  6. Use the Reef Coral Guide to see which corals suit your lighting and water.
  7. Use the Reef Fish Guide and Reef Invert Guide to check size, tank and care needs for the fish, snails, shrimp and crabs you keep, and add photos of your own.

Reef Water Tracker

1. Choose your coral group

Use the chips at the top. Mixed reef uses the narrowest overlap of the Soft, LPS and SPS ranges, so it suits tanks with all three. Soft corals, LPS and SPS use the ranges from the Reef Coral Guide. Fish only is for fish and live rock without corals.

2. Enter your tank

  • Pick a Tank size from the list, or type your own Length, Width and Depth in centimetres. The list switches to Custom size if your numbers don’t match a preset.
  • Add any sump or extra water in litres.
  • If you know your real system volume, type it in total litres. This overrides the size above.
  • The working volume is the tank volume less a flat 10% for rock and sand, plus any sump. Dosing amounts are based on it.

3. Enter your test results

Fill in any readings you have. You don’t need all of them. Set the test date, and choose ppt or SG beside salinity. Changing the unit converts what you’ve typed. The tracker opens with your last saved readings already filled in, so just change the ones you’ve retested.

4. Read the status

  • In range: inside the ideal range. Leave it alone.
  • Low or High: outside ideal but within what the coral tolerates.
  • Very low or Very high: outside the tolerated range. The box turns red.

Ideal is where to aim. Tolerated is what corals can survive for short periods. For alkalinity, 7–11 dKH is the ideal range for every coral group, and each group also has a narrower optimal range inside it, shown in brackets. The advice aims for the optimal range when a reading is out of range.

5. Follow “What to do”

Problems are listed in priority order: ammonia and nitrite first, then temperature, salinity, magnesium, alkalinity, calcium, pH, nitrate and phosphate. Readings outside the tolerated range come before milder ones. Work from the top, and fix magnesium before calcium and alkalinity, because low magnesium stops them holding.

  • Dose amounts are the pure chemical (sodium bicarbonate for alkalinity, calcium chloride for calcium, magnesium chloride for magnesium, all food grade or laboratory grade). For commercial products, follow the label, or use the Reef Water Chemistry calculators, which include Red Sea and Seachem products.
  • Large corrections are split over several days. The tracker never suggests raising alkalinity by more than 1 dKH, calcium by more than 20 ppm, or magnesium by more than 50 ppm in a day, and the same limits apply when it advises water changes to lower a reading. Change salinity by no more than 1 ppt (0.001 SG) and temperature by no more than 1 °C per day.
  • Retest 24 hours after each dose before dosing again.

6. Save to the log

Select Save to log to keep the readings. Saving again on the same date replaces that entry. Open My log to see every entry with all ten parameters, and use Delete to remove one. Use Download backup there to keep a copy, Restore backup to load it again, or Export CSV to open the log in a spreadsheet. If your browser blocks downloads, use Copy backup and paste the text into the box later to restore it. After you have two entries, a How often to dose section estimates daily use and a maintenance dose.

The estimate assumes you added nothing between tests. If you did dose, real use is higher.

Suggested testing

Temperature daily. Salinity, alkalinity, calcium and nitrate weekly. Magnesium and phosphate every two weeks. Ammonia and nitrite after any livestock loss or new additions.

The Coral Guide chip at the top opens the guide filtered to your chosen group.

Reef Water Chemistry

Salinity, magnesium, alkalinity, calcium and pH affect each other. This page shows how, checks your readings and works out how much of each product to add. Use the Reef Water Tracker to keep a log of your results.

1. Choose your coral group

Use the chips at the top: Mixed reef, Soft corals, LPS, SPS or Fish only. They set the ideal ranges and the starting targets for the calculators, and use the same ranges as the Tracker and the Coral Guide.

2. Check my water

Type in any of salinity, magnesium, alkalinity, calcium and pH. You don’t need all five. Choose ppt or SG beside salinity; changing the unit converts what you’ve typed. Each gets an In range, Low or High status. What to do, in order lists the fixes in the right sequence: salinity, then magnesium, then alkalinity, then calcium, then pH. An alkalinity reading inside 7–11 dKH counts as in range, and shows In range, above optimal if it is above the optimal range for your coral group. It also warns you about precipitation risk (high calcium with high alkalinity), a low magnesium to calcium ratio, and whether a pH problem looks like low alkalinity or CO₂.

If you use the Reef Water Tracker on the same device, this page fills itself in from it when it opens: your coral group, salinity unit, system litres, and the salinity, pH, alkalinity, calcium and magnesium from your most recent Save to log entry. It also works out your daily drop for alkalinity, calcium and magnesium from your last two tests, and puts it in the calculators’ Daily drop boxes. A line above the table shows what was filled in. If you test again, save the new readings in the Tracker and select Fill from Reef Water Tracker to reload them. You can still type over any value on this page.

If you open the pages straight from a folder or drive on your computer, the browser may not share the Tracker’s saved data. In that case use Load from Tracker backup file and choose the backup (or CSV export) from My log in the Tracker, or open the pages from a web address.

Your alkalinity, calcium and magnesium readings fill in automatically as the Current values in the calculators below. You can still change them there to try other numbers.

3. How they interact

Short explanations of each parameter: salinity sets the baseline for all the others, magnesium keeps calcium and alkalinity dissolved, alkalinity buffers pH, calcium is the building block, and pH is mostly the result of the rest. Corals use calcium and alkalinity together, about 1 dKH for every 7 ppm of calcium.

4. Calculators: alkalinity, calcium and magnesium

  1. Enter your System litres (tank plus sump) once at the top of the section.
  2. In each calculator, check the Current and Target. The target starts at the middle of the ideal range for your coral group. For alkalinity, 7–11 dKH is acceptable and each group has a narrower optimal range inside it, so the starting target is the middle of the optimal range.
  3. Choose your Product. The list has the common pure chemicals (food-grade or laboratory-grade only, never supermarket, hardware or pool products) plus Red Sea Reef Foundation A, B and C (liquid and powder) and Seachem Reef Builder and Reef Advantage Calcium. Liquids are shown in mL and powders in grams.
  4. Set New salt water reads to what your salt mix measures. It is used when you need to lower a reading with a water change.
  5. Optionally enter a Daily drop, how much you lose per day. The calculator then gives a daily maintenance dose. Your Tracker log estimates this once you have two entries.

To raise a reading you get the total amount to add, split over several days so it never goes above +1 dKH, +20 ppm calcium or +50 ppm magnesium per day. To lower a reading you get a plan of water changes, sized so the reading never falls faster than the same daily limits. The calculators also show the matching dose of the other element, so alkalinity and calcium stay balanced.

5. Balance everything to my calcium

Choose the calcium level you want to hold, then enter your current calcium, alkalinity and magnesium. The page sets matching targets. Magnesium is aimed at about 3.07 times calcium. If your alkalinity or magnesium is already inside its ideal range, it is left alone; if alkalinity is out of range it is aimed at the optimal range for your coral group. You can also type your own alkalinity and magnesium targets in the I want boxes. A table shows each element’s change and what to do. Work down it in order, with calcium and alkalinity on different days, and retest after each water change.

Saved on your device

Your product choices and system litres are remembered on this device. This page doesn’t keep a log of readings; use the Water Tracker for that.

Dose amounts use the manufacturer’s label rates. Always check the label on your own bottle.

Reef Coral Guide

Find corals

Type in the search box to find a coral by name. The chips filter by All, Owned, Soft, LPS, SPS and Saved.

Mark what you keep

  • Tick the box in the corner of a card to say I own this coral. The count shows on the Owned chip.
  • Open a coral and select Save coral to add it to your Saved list.
  • Keep more than one type of the same coral, such as several zoanthid morphs? Open the coral and use My varieties to add each one by name. Adding a variety marks the coral as owned, and your varieties show on its card and in the search box.

Open a coral

Tap a card to see its type, care level, lighting, flow, feeding, behaviour and placement. It also shows suggested position and light for your setup, which corals can sit close and which need a gap, and its ideal and tolerated water parameters. Check my water against these ranges opens the Water Tracker on that coral’s group.

Add your own photo

Open a coral and select Add my photo. On a phone you can take a picture or choose one from your library. Your photo replaces the drawing on that coral’s card. Use Change my photo or Remove photo to update it. Photos are shrunk to save space.

Set your lighting and tank

Open My lighting. Choose your light, the number of lights and the intensity. Pick a Tank size or type the Length, Width and Depth in centimetres. The guide estimates light at different depths so you can place corals where they will get what they need. Choose Custom and enter a reading from a PAR meter for the most accurate result.

Light estimates are rough. Height, lens, tank size and settings change them a lot.

Reef Fish Guide

Find fish

Type in the search box to find a fish by common or scientific name. The chips filter by All, Owned and each group: clownfish, gobies and blennies, wrasses, tangs, angelfish, basslets and dottybacks, cardinalfish and others, damsels and chromis, and butterflyfish.

Mark what you keep

Tick the box in the corner of a card, or in a fish’s details, to say I own this fish. The count shows on the Owned chip, and the Owned chip shows only your fish.

Open a fish

Tap a card to see its adult size, minimum tank size in litres, care level, diet, temperament and whether it is reef safe, plus a care tip and the typical water ranges. Check my water opens the Water Tracker on the Mixed reef group. Minimum tanks are general guidance, and some fish are marked With caution because they may nip at corals or eat small shrimp.

Add your own photos

Open a fish and select Add my photo. On a phone you can take a picture or choose one from your library. You can add up to four photos for each fish. Your first photo replaces the drawing on that fish’s card. Use Remove photo to delete one. Photos are shrunk to save space and stay in this browser.

Back up your owned list

Open My saved data at the bottom of the page. Copy backup copies a short text of your owned fish, and Restore backup brings them back when you paste it in. Photos are not part of this backup.

Illustrations are stylised placeholders, not photographs. Always quarantine new fish for several weeks and research each species before you buy.

Reef Invert Guide

Find inverts

Type in the search box to find an invert by common or scientific name. The chips filter by All, Owned and each group: snails, shrimp, crabs and hermits, urchins and starfish, and clams and worms.

Mark what you keep

Tick the box in the corner of a card, or in an animal’s details, to say I own this invert. The Owned chip shows a count and lists only your inverts.

Open an invert

Tap a card to see its adult size, minimum tank size in litres, care level, diet or role, temperament and whether it is reef safe, plus a care tip and the water it needs. The water table includes calcium, alkalinity and magnesium, because shells and skeletons use them. A single note at the top of the page reminds you never to use copper-based medications in a tank with inverts. Check my water opens the Water Tracker on the Mixed reef group. Some inverts are marked With caution, for example an emerald crab that may catch small fish if underfed.

Add your own photos

Open an invert and select Add my photo. You can add up to four photos for each one. Your first photo replaces the drawing on its card. Use Remove photo to delete one. Photos are shrunk to save space and stay in this browser.

Back up your owned list

Open My saved data at the bottom of the page. Copy backup copies a short text of your owned inverts, and Restore backup brings them back when you paste it in. Photos are not part of this backup.

Illustrations are stylised placeholders, not photographs. Inverts are sensitive to sudden changes in salinity, temperature and water chemistry, so acclimate them slowly.

Your data and backups

All five pages save automatically on the device and browser you used. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere, and there’s no account. The one outside request is the pages loading their typefaces from Google Fonts.

  • A different browser or device starts empty.
  • Clearing your browser’s site data erases your log, tank settings, photos and selections.
  • The Reef Coral Guide has a My saved data section. Use Download backup file or Copy backup to keep a copy of your owned corals, saved corals, varieties, lighting and tank size, and Restore backup to bring them back. Photos are not part of that backup.
  • The Reef Water Tracker log has its own backup. In My log, use Download backup and Restore backup. Restoring replaces any entries with the same date. Tank settings are not included.
  • The Reef Water Chemistry page remembers only your chosen products and system litres. It has no log or backup.
  • The Reef Fish Guide and Reef Invert Guide remember your owned animals and photos in this browser. Their backups cover the owned list only, not photos.
  • In the Reef Coral Guide, Chrome and Edge on a computer can also save automatically to a file you choose.
  • If you open the pages straight from a folder or drive on your computer, some browsers keep each page’s saved data separate, so the Water Chemistry page may not see your Tracker log. Open the pages from a web address, or use Load from Tracker backup file on the Chemistry page.

If you rely on your log, download a backup now and then. Export CSV gives you a copy you can open in a spreadsheet.

General guidance only, not a substitute for advice from an aquatic professional. Retest after every change and adjust slowly.
