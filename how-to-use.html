How to use these tools

Two pages that work together: the Reef Water Tracker checks your test results, and the Reef Coral Guide helps you choose and place corals.

Quick start

  1. Open the Reef Water Tracker and choose the coral group that matches your tank.
  2. Check your tank size and type in your latest test results.
  3. Follow the numbered list under What to do, starting at number 1.
  4. Select Save to log so the tracker can learn how fast your tank uses calcium, alkalinity and magnesium.
  5. Use the Reef Coral Guide to see which corals suit your lighting and water.

Reef Water Tracker

1. Choose your coral group

Use the chips at the top. Mixed reef uses the narrowest overlap of the Soft, LPS and SPS ranges, so it suits tanks with all three. Soft corals, LPS and SPS use the ranges from the Reef Coral Guide. Fish only is for fish and live rock without corals.

2. Enter your tank

  • Pick a Tank size from the list, or type your own Length, Width and Depth in centimetres. The list switches to Custom size if your numbers don’t match a preset.
  • Add any sump or extra water in litres.
  • If you know your real system volume, type it in total litres. This overrides the size above.
  • The working volume is the tank volume less a flat 10% for rock and sand, plus any sump. Dosing amounts are based on it.

3. Enter your test results

Fill in any readings you have. You don’t need all of them. Set the test date, and choose ppt or SG beside salinity. Changing the unit converts what you’ve typed. The tracker opens with your last saved readings already filled in, so just change the ones you’ve retested.

4. Read the status

  • In range: inside the ideal range. Leave it alone.
  • Low or High: outside ideal but within what the coral tolerates.
  • Very low or Very high: outside the tolerated range. The box turns red.

Ideal is where to aim. Tolerated is what corals can survive for short periods.

5. Follow “What to do”

Problems are listed in priority order: ammonia and nitrite first, then temperature, salinity, magnesium, alkalinity, calcium, pH, nitrate and phosphate. Readings outside the tolerated range come before milder ones. Work from the top, and fix magnesium before calcium and alkalinity, because low magnesium stops them holding.

  • Dose amounts are the pure chemical (baking soda for alkalinity, calcium chloride for calcium, magnesium chloride for magnesium). For commercial products, follow the label.
  • Large corrections are split over several days. The tracker never suggests raising alkalinity by more than 1 dKH, calcium by more than 20 ppm, or magnesium by more than 50 ppm in a day. Change salinity by no more than 1 ppt (0.001 SG) and temperature by no more than 1 °C per day.
  • Retest 24 hours after each dose before dosing again.

6. Save to the log

Select Save to log to keep the readings. Saving again on the same date replaces that entry. Open My log to see every entry with all ten parameters, and use Delete to remove one. Use Download backup there to keep a copy, Restore backup to load it again, or Export CSV to open the log in a spreadsheet. After you have two entries, a How often to dose section estimates daily use and a maintenance dose.

The estimate assumes you added nothing between tests. If you did dose, real use is higher.

Suggested testing

Temperature daily. Salinity, alkalinity, calcium and nitrate weekly. Magnesium and phosphate every two weeks. Ammonia and nitrite after any livestock loss or new additions.

The Coral Guide chip at the top opens the guide filtered to your chosen group.

Reef Coral Guide

Find corals

Type in the search box to find a coral by name. The chips filter by All, Owned, Soft, LPS, SPS and Saved.

Mark what you keep

  • Tick the box in the corner of a card to say I own this coral. The count shows on the Owned chip.
  • Open a coral and select Save coral to add it to your Saved list.
  • Keep more than one type of the same coral, such as several zoanthid morphs? Open the coral and use My varieties to add each one by name. Adding a variety marks the coral as owned, and your varieties show on its card and in the search box.

Open a coral

Tap a card to see its type, care level, lighting, flow, feeding, behaviour and placement. It also shows suggested position and light for your setup, which corals can sit close and which need a gap, and its ideal and tolerated water parameters. Check my water against these ranges opens the Water Tracker on that coral’s group.

Add your own photo

Open a coral and select Add my photo. On a phone you can take a picture or choose one from your library. Your photo replaces the drawing on that coral’s card. Use Change my photo or Remove photo to update it. Photos are shrunk to save space.

Set your lighting and tank

Open My lighting. Choose your light, the number of lights and the intensity. Pick a Tank size or type the Length, Width and Depth in centimetres. The guide estimates light at different depths so you can place corals where they will get what they need. Choose Custom and enter a reading from a PAR meter for the most accurate result.

Light estimates are rough. Height, lens, tank size and settings change them a lot.

Your data and backups

Both pages save automatically on the device and browser you used. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere, and there’s no account. The one outside request is the pages loading their typefaces from Google Fonts.

  • A different browser or device starts empty.
  • Clearing your browser’s site data erases your log, tank settings, photos and selections.
  • The Reef Coral Guide has a My saved data section. Use Download backup file or Copy backup to keep a copy of your owned corals, saved corals, varieties, lighting and tank size, and Restore backup to bring them back. Photos are not part of that backup.
  • The Reef Water Tracker log has its own backup. In My log, use Download backup and Restore backup. Restoring replaces any entries with the same date. Tank settings are not included.
  • On a computer, Chrome and Edge can also save automatically to a file you choose.

If you rely on your log, download a backup now and then. Export CSV gives you a copy you can open in a spreadsheet.

General guidance only, not a substitute for advice from an aquatic professional. Retest after every change and adjust slowly.
