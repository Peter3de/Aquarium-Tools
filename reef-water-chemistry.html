Reef Water Chemistry

Salinity, magnesium, alkalinity (dKH), calcium and pH are linked. Fix them in the wrong order and the numbers drift back or the chemistry crashes out as white powder. This page explains how they interact, checks your readings and works out doses.

2. How they interact

The fix-it order

Salinity→Magnesium→Alkalinity→Calcium→pH

Each step makes the next one hold. Don’t chase calcium or alkalinity while salinity or magnesium is off.

Salinity: the baseline

Calcium, magnesium and alkalinity are all concentrations, so they rise and fall with salinity. A tank at 38 ppt reads high on everything; one at 30 ppt reads low. Test salinity first and calibrate your refractometer with 35 ppt (1.026 SG) fluid.

Evaporation removes only water, so top up with RODI and never salt water. Keep swings small: under 1 ppt (about 0.001 SG) per day.

Magnesium: the stabiliser

Magnesium keeps calcium and carbonate dissolved. When it is low (below about 1250 ppm), calcium and alkalinity precipitate onto heaters, pumps and glass, so they drop even after you dose and never hold.

Ideal is roughly three times your calcium (a Mg:Ca ratio near 3:1). It is used slowly, so it needs dosing far less often.

Alkalinity (dKH): the buffer

Alkalinity is the carbonate and bicarbonate that holds pH steady and that corals use to build skeletons. It is the fastest-consumed parameter in a stony-coral tank and the one that swings most. Avoid changes of more than 1 dKH a day.

Higher alkalinity raises pH; low alkalinity lets pH fall and wobble overnight.

Calcium: the building block

Corals take up calcium and alkalinity together, in a fixed ratio: about 1 dKH for every 7 ppm of calcium. If you dose them in that ratio, both stay steady. If one drifts away from the other, dose just the one that is lagging.

Calcium alone barely moves pH, but pushing calcium and alkalinity both high at once is what triggers precipitation.

pH: the result

pH is mostly an outcome, not a dial. It is set by alkalinity, plus the CO₂ in your room air. Low pH with good alkalinity usually means high CO₂: ventilate the room or run the skimmer from outside air. High pH after dosing means too much alkalinity or kalkwasser too quickly.

It normally dips at night when lights are off, so test at the same time each day.

The precipitation trap

Seawater is close to saturated with calcium carbonate. High calcium plus high alkalinity plus low magnesium plus warm water is the recipe for snowstorms, scaly pumps and sudden drops. Dose alkalinity and calcium separately, slowly, in well-mixed flow, and never both into the same spot at once.

3. Calculators: alkalinity, calcium, magnesium

Current readings fill in from Check my water above, or you can type them here. Targets start at the middle of the ideal range for the coral group chosen at the top; change them if you like. Each calculator works out a raise (grams to add) or a lower (water change), and a daily maintenance dose if you enter how much you lose per day. Use food-grade or laboratory-grade chemicals only.

Amounts are pure-chemical equivalents. Per litre: 0.030 g sodium bicarbonate per dKH, 0.0189 g anhydrous sodium carbonate per dKH, 3.67 mg calcium chloride dihydrate (2.77 mg anhydrous) per ppm calcium, 8.36 mg magnesium chloride hexahydrate (10.1 mg magnesium sulfate heptahydrate) per ppm magnesium. Use only food-grade or laboratory-grade chemicals, not supermarket, hardware or pool products such as baking soda, washing soda, Epsom salt or de-icer, which can contain additives or impurities. Red Sea Foundation A, B and C use the label rates: liquid, 1 mL per 100 L raises calcium 2 ppm, alkalinity 0.1 dKH or magnesium 1 ppm; powder, 1 g per 100 L raises calcium 3.6 ppm, alkalinity 0.33 dKH or magnesium 1.34 ppm. Seachem Reef Builder uses 3 g per 150 L for +0.25 meq/L (0.7 dKH), and Reef Advantage Calcium about 5 g per 150 L for +12 ppm. Always check the label on your own bottle. Maximum steps per day: 1 dKH, 20 ppm calcium, 50 ppm magnesium.

Otherwise alkalinity moves to the nearest sensible point in the range, and magnesium to about 3.07 times calcium (the natural seawater ratio) kept inside the range. Anything you type in the “I want” boxes is used instead.

Keeping balanced day to day

Test alkalinity and calcium weekly, magnesium every two weeks, salinity and temperature every day. Note how many dKH you lose per day. For every 1 dKH you replace, replace about 7 ppm of calcium. If calcium stays level while alkalinity falls, your calcium is coming from salt water changes: dose alkalinity only. Use the Reef Water Tracker to log readings and see your daily consumption.

Guidelines only. Change parameters slowly, retest after each adjustment and confirm odd readings with a second test kit. SG and ppt are converted at about 25 °C.
